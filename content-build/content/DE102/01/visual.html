1. The Dependency Map

From Tables, Rows and Keys you know a key uniquely identifies a row. A functional dependency is the same idea with the key requirement removed: X determines Y. Tap each dependency and watch where redundancy appears — it is always the same place. Like a form where one answer forces another: postcode determines city, so writing the city on every line adds nothing but the chance of typing it differently.

order_lines PRIMARY KEY (order_id, line_no) order_id · line_no · customer_id · customer_email · product_sku · product_name · qty · unit_price

One sentence predicts every normal-form violation you will ever find: redundancy appears exactly where a dependency's left side is not a key. Everything after this panel is that rule applied in three specific shapes.

2. Key, Whole Key, Nothing But

Three normal forms, three specific violations, three splits. Step through and watch one wide table become four narrow ones — and watch what stays behind on purpose. Like unpacking a suitcase where everything was rolled inside everything else.

Higher forms exist — BCNF closes an edge case where a non-key column determines part of a key — but 3NF is the working target for a transactional schema and the returns diminish fast past it. The transferable skill is not the forms. It is the question they force you to answer about every single column: which entity does this fact describe?

3. Three Anomalies

Redundancy would cost only disk if data never changed. It changes. Pick a scenario and run it against both schemas. Like writing your phone number on 812 forms and then moving house.

This is the deep claim of 3NF, and it is stronger than "tidier": each fact lives in one row of one table, so an update is a single-row write that any transactional store makes atomic. Inconsistency stops being discouraged and becomes structurally unrepresentable.

4. Paying Storage For Reads

A join is a computation you run on every read. Denormalization moves it to write time, where you run it once. Drag the read volume and watch the trade flip. Like pre-chopping vegetables: pointless for one meal, obvious for a hundred.

Safe only under three conditions

Experts denormalize the serving layer and keep the system of record normalized. The amateur mistake is denormalizing the only copy and then hand-patching it — which is the update anomaly with a process wrapped around it.

5. Three Shapes

One order with three lines, stored three ways. Watch what each shape duplicates, what it costs to read, and what it costs to change. Like keeping receipts in separate envelopes, taped end to end, or folded inside the order they belong to.

Choose nesting when children arrive with their parent and are queried with it — an order's lines are created in the same event and rarely mean anything alone. Choose a child table when children are first-class: queried standalone across many parents, updated independently, or shared between parents. Followed to its endpoint this design gives the "one big table" per analytical domain — and it is the shape your corpus tables will echo.
