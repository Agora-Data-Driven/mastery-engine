1. Measure Or Context

Normalization and Denormalization asked which entity a fact describes. Here the question narrows: is this column something you measure, or something you slice by? Tap each column of a raw sales export and sort it. Like a receipt: the amounts are what you add up, everything else is how you find it again.

Start from the business process and its measurement event, then ask what context describes it. Starting from a list of source tables reproduces operational complexity and calls it a model.

2. What Sums, What Does Not

Three measures, two directions to add them up. One works everywhere, one works in only one direction, and one must never be added at all. Like adding up bank balances: across accounts today, fine; across days for one account, nonsense.

Why a stored ratio can never be repaired downstream

Three fact patterns, chosen by the business process: a transaction fact appends one row per event; a periodic snapshot records state at an interval (which is where semi-additive measures come from); an accumulating snapshot updates milestone columns as one process instance moves through its stages.

3. Declare The Grain

Grain is the exact statement of what one row represents, written before you choose a single column. Pick a candidate and see whether it survives the data — and what questions it just made unanswerable. Like deciding whether a photo album has one page per holiday, per day, or per photograph.

Write the grain statement in the model documentation and encode it in tests: uniqueness of the row key, not-null on grain columns, accepted event types, reconciliation totals. And before adding any column, ask whether its value is functionally determined at the declared grain — a line quantity is; a customer's current segment is not, unless it comes through a time-aware dimension.

4. Mixed Grain Overcounts

An order-level number sitting on a line-level row is the most damaging defect a warehouse can carry, because nothing errors and the error factor differs per order. Drag the line count. Like writing the delivery fee on every item in the box and then adding up the box.

Three repairs

Late-arriving and duplicated events test the same contract. Pipelines should carry stable source identifiers or idempotency keys, so a payment delivered twice does not double count — and when a correction arrives the model must already say whether it replaces, compensates, or versions.

5. Star Or Snowflake

One million products, 2,000 brands, 500 categories. Flatten the hierarchy into the dimension, or normalise it out? Tap a consideration and read what each shape actually costs. Like a contacts app: full address on every card, or a link to a shared address book.

The best schema is the simplest model that preserves the declared grain and the history while meeting the workload. A star is often the default because analytical systems favour reads and columnar compression. Snowflake selectively, where independent governance, genuine reuse, or real structural complexity justifies the extra join path — and hybrids are normal.
