1. Four Grains, One Chain

Grain: What One Row Represents and Document Versioning as SCD Type 2 are the whole toolkit; a corpus is four of them chained. Tap a layer to see what one of its rows means — then fire an upgrade and watch which layers are invalidated. Like a printing press: the manuscript, the edition, the pages, the photographs of the pages.

Fire an upgrade — watch what has to be rebuilt

Processing runs deserve a grain of their own too: an ingestion run, parse attempt, chunking run or embedding batch, each with status, configuration, errors and counts. processed = true on the document cannot express a retry or a partial failure — and a run-to-artifact relationship is what makes audit and idempotent recovery possible.

2. What A Chunk Costs

Chunking chooses what one retrieval row means, so it sets recall, citation precision, vector count and token cost all at once. Drag the window and the stride over a 6,000-token policy document. Like choosing how finely to slice bread: too thin and it falls apart, too thick and you cannot taste the filling.

Changing chunk grain is a schema migration, not a config tweak: build the new chunk set under a new version, embed it, validate coverage, switch the serving alias, keep rollback. Updating rows in place leaves a live index mixing incompatible boundaries and ids, which returns overlapping and missing passages at the same time and reads as a relevance problem.

3. Identity Under Change

Someone inserts one sentence into the introduction. What happens next depends entirely on what your chunk ids were derived from. Like numbering the pages of a book versus naming each chapter: insert a page and one of those survives.

The exact recipe depends on whether identity should represent occurrence (this span, in this version) or content (this text, wherever it appears) — or both. What is never defensible is deriving it from a position that any edit can shift.

4. The Hash Recipe

A content hash is evidence of equality under a canonicalisation rule. Change the rule and you change which documents count as the same. Toggle each ingredient and watch two inputs collide or separate. Like deciding whether "St." and "Street" are the same address.

embedding_id = hash("embedding-v1", chunk_id, model_id, preprocessing_hash) chunk_id = hash("chunk-v2", version_id, chunker_config_hash, start, end, text_hash) text_hash = hash("canon-v3", canonicalized_bytes)

Mutable pointers still have a job. A serving alias points from logical document to current version, or from model family to active index. The immutable ids preserve history; the pointer carries current behaviour — and updating a pointer is far safer than rewriting every artifact's identity.

5. Tracing A Citation

A retrieved chunk becomes a trustworthy citation only if you can walk back from the vector to a real span in a real source. Follow the path — then break a link and see what the citation becomes. Like a footnote that names the edition and the page, not just the book.

Break something

Lineage is an explanation boundary, not a truth claim. It can prove which source and which transformation produced an artifact. It cannot prove the source is true or the answer correct — that needs relevance evaluation, source-quality policy and runtime grounding checks alongside it. A trustworthy corpus makes origin verifiable and uncertainty visible.
