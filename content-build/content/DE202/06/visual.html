1. Retry Meets Idempotency

The orchestrator's most basic feature is the retry — and it is exactly the thing lesson 2 warned you about. A task dies halfway through writing a partition. Choose what the task does on its next attempt. Like a builder who either finishes the half-built wall or knocks it down and starts clean.

Nothing here is new — it is lesson 2's idempotent write, now triggered by something you did not choose. That is the point: the orchestrator will re-run your task, so the retry policy is only as safe as the write path underneath it.

2. Edges Cost Time

Two dimension builds depend on the same raw snapshot. Add an edge between them and watch the critical path. Like two cooks who could work at once, told to take turns.

The opposite mistake costs correctness rather than time: omit a true dependency and the fact table publishes before its dimensions are ready. Edges should encode data readiness — never incidental code order.

3. Green But Empty

Four runs. Every one of them exited zero. Tap each to see what the orchestrator knew, and what it could not possibly know. Like a delivery marked "completed" with an empty box on the doorstep.

4. Threshold Goldilocks

Yesterday: 4.0M rows. Drag today's count and see which rule fires. Like a smoke alarm: too sensitive and you disable it, too dull and it never wakes you.

Severity is a design decision, not a property of the test. Classify each as blocking, warning or informational — and make warning accumulation observable, or a slow drift becomes permanent background noise nobody reads.

5. Three Separate Decisions

Shipping code, computing history, and making data visible. Fuse them into one release and you get one irreversible event. Step through the durable pattern and see what each stage buys back. Like a stage play: rehearse, run a preview, then open the doors.
