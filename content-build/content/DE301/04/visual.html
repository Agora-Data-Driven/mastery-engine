1. From Features To Text

Everything this course taught about feature pipelines — named stages, versions, "as of when", rebuildable datasets — is the same discipline applied to far messier raw material. Tap a familiar idea and see where it lands in a text pipeline. Like moving from a tidy spreadsheet to a shoebox of receipts: the accounting rules do not change, the inputs just fight back.

The through-line: each stage changes what downstream models can learn and retrieve. Separating the stages and recording their versions is what makes false removals, parser defects and distribution shifts diagnosable instead of irreversible — which is exactly why you versioned datasets alongside models in the first place.

2. Parse Or Quarantine

Extraction fails constantly, and the only question that matters is whether it fails loudly. Pick a format, then choose what the stage does when it cannot parse. Like a courier who either leaves a "we tried" card or quietly drops the parcel in a hedge.

Two things the policy cannot fix on its own. Source offsets: normalized character positions may not map to HTML bytes or PDF coordinates, so preserve page and block identifiers, DOM paths, bounding boxes or span mappings — a plain text blob cannot cite its evidence. Resource limits: cap decompressed size, page count, recursion depth, OCR time and memory per artifact, record a specific limit-exceeded outcome instead of retrying forever, and parse untrusted formats in isolated workers with restricted network and filesystem permissions.

3. MinHash And SimHash

Exact hashes only catch byte-identical copies. These two build a compact signature whose agreement estimates a similarity you actually care about. Switch method, then drag the agreement. Like recognising a song from eight bars rather than comparing the whole recording.

Neither signature proves semantic equivalence — two contradictory documents sharing a template can look near-duplicate. And containment is not symmetric: a short article copied inside a long compilation has low Jaccard despite substantial reuse, so compare containment ratios or segment the long document, and retain the metric and threshold in pair provenance.

4. Bands And Rows

All-pairs comparison is impossible at corpus scale, so LSH buckets signatures and only compares what collides. Two dials shape the curve that decides what collides. Like a sieve: the mesh size decides what falls through, and you can only pick one mesh.

The LSH stage generates candidates only — a verification stage still calculates Jaccard, Hamming distance or another exact criterion. Watch the buckets too: empty documents, common boilerplate and repeated default pages make buckets go hot. Cap or special-case them, never discard them silently — a huge bucket may be a real template family, or a parsing bug producing identical empty text.

5. Funnel With Reasons

Every filter encodes a value judgment, so the corpus report is the product. Move the quality threshold and watch which subcorpora survive — then give each document type its own threshold instead. Like one dress code for a wedding, a construction site and a swimming pool.

The final corpus report should state source coverage, parsing success, duplicate rates, language distribution, quality retention, quarantine volume and known blind spots. A smaller corpus with explained provenance and measured filters is often more useful than a larger opaque collection whose missing voices cannot be diagnosed. Appeals need governance too: a trusted source rejected by a generic rule can get a scoped exception — with owner, rationale and expiry recorded — but a global allowlist that bypasses every later safety and quality check is a permanent blind spot.
