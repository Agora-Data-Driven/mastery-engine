The judgment layer: default to the cheapest service whose design center you are inside, and escalate only on a demonstrated axis violation. Violating the matrix does not fail loudly — it degrades. Analytics on Cloud SQL starves OLTP traffic; point lookups on BigQuery cost a full column scan and hundreds of milliseconds; blobs in a relational database bloat backups and replication. Nothing throws an error.

2. The Relational Ladder

All three speak SQL with ACID transactions. They differ in how far writes scale and how availability is achieved — so each requirement you switch on knocks a rung out. Toggle the requirements, or load a scenario. Like choosing a vehicle: a van, a van with a bigger engine, or a freight network — you only pay for the network when the load will not fit in one van.

The exam's tell for Spanner is the co-occurrence of relational + global + strong consistency. Any two of those alone are usually cheaper elsewhere, and the price floor is far above Cloud SQL. Read the escalation clause literally: "zero data loss through a regional outage" rules out Cloud SQL HA, which is failover to a standby in a second zone.

3. Three NoSQL Centers

Firestore, Bigtable and Memorystore each drop relational joins to buy a different superpower, and they are not interchangeable. Pick a piece of one RAG chat product, then drag how much a single query returns and watch the cost model bite. Like a filing cabinet, a warehouse aisle and a coat pocket: the same papers, three completely different retrieval habits.

Judgment: Firestore's per-operation pricing punishes scan-heavy workloads that Bigtable serves cheaply; Bigtable's minimum node cost and key-design burden are wasted on a 100k-document app that Firestore runs serverlessly; and anything you cannot afford to lose does not live in Memorystore — snapshots and AOF are optional, not a durability contract.

4. The Temperature Ladder

All four GCS classes carry 11 nines of durability. What changes is retrieval economics and a minimum storage duration — so the axis is access frequency, never importance. Drag how often you read 10 TB back, then drag an object's age through a lifecycle policy. Like a fridge, a freezer and a deep-freeze in the garage: the further back you put it, the cheaper it is to keep and the more of a trek it is to fetch.

Autoclass inverts the whole approach: the bucket observes each object's access pattern and moves it between classes itself, charging no retrieval fees but a small per-object management fee — the right default when access is unpredictable or per-prefix rules would sprawl. And keep the staging substrate Standard: raw documents awaiting embedding, Dataflow temp files, BigQuery load and export files, model artifacts. Lifecycle-manage the historical tail only.

5. Choosing The Mover

Four movers, and the exam scenario always names the same four facts: volume, network capacity, freshness requirement, destination. Set them and the service falls out mechanically — including the arithmetic that makes a truck faster than a fibre. Like posting a hard drive versus emailing the files: past a certain size the van on the motorway really is quicker.

The distinction that decides the most questions is intent, not mechanism: DMS and Datastream both replicate continuously, but DMS ends with the source retired, while Datastream runs forever keeping a warehouse or lake synchronized. Log-based CDC also avoids the load of repeated SELECT polling and captures deletes, which polling misses entirely.
