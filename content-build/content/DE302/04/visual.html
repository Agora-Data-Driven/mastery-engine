1. Move Once Or Stream

Migration Services: Datastream, DMS, BigQuery Data Transfer, Transfer Appliance moved a finite dataset in once, and The Workload-Driven Storage Decision Matrix taught you to choose from the shape of the work. Tap a requirement and watch which side of that line it falls on. Like the difference between a house move and a mail subscription: one ends, the other never does.

The one number the managed service does not manage for you

A one-time move has no such clock. A continuous stream does, and it belongs to the source database, not to Google Cloud — which is the whole reason "managed CDC" still needs an operator. Everything after this visual assumes data that keeps arriving.

2. Copies Not Cursors

A topic is the publish point. A subscription is a durable, named delivery path with its own copy and its own cursor. Move alerting between the two wirings and watch how many events it actually sees. Like a magazine: every household gets its own copy, but four flatmates sharing one copy each read a quarter of it.

The rule underneath: subscription design mirrors consumer responsibility. One subscription shared among instances of the SAME logical consumer is load balancing and is correct. One subscription shared between DIFFERENT applications is competition, and nothing anywhere reports it as an error — you find it when an alert never fires.

3. Ack And Redelivery

Standard delivery is at least once, so the acknowledgement is the contract. Choose where you ack, turn exactly-once on or off, then break something. Like signing for a parcel: sign before you open the box and a missing item is your problem forever.

Exactly-once delivery is a real feature with a narrow edge: for pull subscriptions in its documented regional scope it gives acknowledgement success information and stops redelivery of a message ID that was already acked. It is not a transaction across your database, and it cannot see that your publisher sent the same business event twice. Idempotency is still the design.

4. Workers That Cannot Help

10,000 events per second arrive; one Dataflow worker sustains 500. Raise the worker maximum and watch how often it changes nothing at all, because something else is the binding constraint. Like hiring more cashiers for a shop with two doors.

Streaming Engine moves much of the execution and state off the worker VMs into the service backend, which cuts persistent disk and makes autoscaling react faster — it does not invent parallelism that the pipeline does not have. And notice what the CPU graph would show in the capped cases: workers idling politely while the backlog grows.

5. Kafka Or Pub/Sub

Both transport event streams; the abstractions and the ownership differ. Tap a dimension to compare, then flip who runs the cluster and watch how few rows actually move. Like owning a car versus a car subscription: the driving is identical, the responsibilities are not.

At awareness level, neither system is universally superior. Calculate total cost of ownership, operational skills, recovery objectives, latency, throughput, data locality and ecosystem dependencies. The correct choice is the one whose guarantees and responsibilities match the product — and a migration is semantic mapping, not API translation.
