1. Six Stages, One Document

Nothing here is new machinery — every stage was built in an earlier course. What is new is the conveyor between them. Tap a stage to see which course gave it to you, what it holds, and where the 40-page PDF is at that moment. Like a factory whose machines you already own: this lesson is the floor plan that connects them.

Each stage carries four labels: the storage system it lives in, the transform that produces it, the team that owns it, and its freshness SLA. That is not bureaucracy — the next lesson measures this path, the cost lesson prices it and the backfill lesson replays it. You cannot monitor, price or rebuild a stage you cannot name.

2. Batch Or Streaming

One real product: 8,000 document edits a day, 40 ACL-or-deletion events, 300 price-sheet updates. Put each one on a path and watch the bill and the exposure move in opposite directions. Like posting a letter versus sending a courier — you do not courier the newsletter, and you never post the keys back.

The rule is freshness requirement × volume × cost, decided per hop — but one input is not a preference. Revocation is a security property, so the 40 ACL events go streaming whatever they cost. Evaluation datasets and analytics are batch, full stop.

3. Which Write Wins

A slow batch backfill replays document d-7712 at version 4 while a streaming update has already written version 6. Slide the backfill's arrival and switch the rule. Like two people editing the same shared document: you settle it by the draft number written on the page, not by who hit save last.

This is why every upsert carries a monotonic version and why identity is deterministic: reprocessing the same input yields the same doc_id and the same chunk_id, so a replay is idempotent instead of destructive. The whole backfill playbook in lesson 4 stands on this one property.

4. Contracts On Arrows

Seven contracts, each with four parts: schema, semantics, SLA, failure behavior. Tap one to open it, then run the two scenarios and watch which arrows hold. Like plumbing to a standard thread: you can swap any fitting for a better one, right up until someone solders straight through the joint.

An SLA per contract — ingestion lag, parse success rate, chunk coverage, embedding freshness, index visibility lag, query p95, answer p95 — is what makes the next lesson possible. A contract without an SLA is a hope, and a contract without a stated failure behavior quietly becomes "drop it and carry on", which destroys the coverage numbers you monitor with.

5. Wipe And Rebuild

The ruthless test: any derived store may be wiped and rebuilt from truth without information loss. Tap stores to wipe them, then turn on the lossy parser and run it again. Like negatives and prints — you can reprint any photo you like, right up until you discover one print had no negative.

Disaster recovery is therefore a backup of the truth plus a rebuild runbook — the copies need no backups at all. Embeddings are the one nuance: derived, but expensive to recompute, so keeping an old generation is a cost decision for lesson 3 to price, never a truth question.
