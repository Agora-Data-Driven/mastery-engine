The table above is the whole curriculum compressed into a lookup — and the discipline it enforces is negative, not positive: before touching any layer, name the evidence that would convict it, and refuse to spend effort on layers the evidence has already cleared.

2. Chain Of Custody

Users report that the new refund policy never appears in answers. One trace ID, seven checks, and every one of them either clears a layer or convicts it. Step through it and watch the suspect list shrink. Like tracking a parcel: you do not guess where it went, you read the last scan that still shows it.

The whole walk cost minutes and the fix is an afternoon of depth and context-k tuning. The plausible wrong move — re-embedding the corpus — costs a week and changes nothing, because the evidence was reaching the system the entire time. Mis-triage is how that week gets spent.

3. The Binding Stage

Three real slowdowns. Pick one, read what each stage actually measured, then spend your week on a fix — and see whether it buys anything. Work on a stage that never moved returns exactly zero. Like widening a road that is not the traffic jam.

One more phenomenon belongs in every latency review: queueing. A stage's p95 includes time spent waiting for a worker, not just working — so a stage whose p50 is flat while its p95 climbs is saturated, not slow, and the answer is capacity or admission control, not optimisation. The three scenarios here are algorithmic; plenty of real incidents are a reranker pool one replica short at peak.

4. Tokens In Tokens Out

Generation is two problems wearing one name. Prefill answers to the tokens you send in; decode answers to the tokens you ask for back. Drag both and watch which number you were actually paying. Like a phone call: how long you wait for someone to pick up, versus how long they talk.

The second lever on prefill is not the slider: chunk wrappers stripped of redundant metadata shrink the same 10 chunks. And note what scenario C actually bought — capping output and streaming collapsed perceived latency while total compute barely changed. Perceived latency is a real number to the user and an almost free one to you.

5. Defending The Choice

A reviewer asks why the system is built this way. Pick the challenge, then flip between the two answers you could give — and watch the reviewer's checklist respond. Like the difference between "I like this restaurant" and "it is open, it is nine minutes away, and it seats twelve".

reviewer's checklist

Every defense ends the same way: with the condition that would reverse it. If multilingual traffic crosses thirty percent, re-embed on the newer model. If retrieval p95 crosses 800 milliseconds, shard. A decision defended by preference is opinion; defended by constraints, measurements and reversal conditions, it is engineering — and the backbone, in the end, is not the diagram from lesson one but the set of decisions you can defend this way.
