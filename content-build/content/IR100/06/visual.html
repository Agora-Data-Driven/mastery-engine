1. Postings To Adjacency

You already store, per term, only the documents that contain it — Linked Lists as Postings and Arrays vs Linked Lists for Postings Storage. A proximity graph stores, per vector, only the few vectors near it. Same shape, different query: one gets intersected, one gets walked. Switch the representation and drag the corpus size. Like a contacts app that keeps each person's handful of friends, instead of a yes/no table of everyone against everyone.

Flat arrays are excellent to traverse and expensive to edit — an insert in the middle shifts everything after it — so builders reserve fixed neighbor capacity or rebuild immutable segments, and deletion leaves a tombstone until compaction. All of it needs stable integer vertex IDs, with an external map from document ID to integer.

2. Walk To The Query

Enter somewhere, score the neighbors, move to the best one if it improves, repeat. Step the worked traversal and watch every distance get computed. Like asking for directions door to door: each neighbour points you one street closer, and you stop when nobody can.

Keep a visited set or a cycle re-does work forever. And note what greedy never did: prove that some unvisited vertex is farther than its answer. That is the whole reason graph search is called approximate — it is a navigation result, not a proof.

3. Scan Or Walk

A graph does not delete search effort, it exposes a dial between exhaustive scoring and one fragile path. Move the corpus, the visit budget, the degree and the dimension, and watch the two costs cross. Like ringing every flat in the city versus following three sets of directions — the second only wins while the city is big.

Bytes matter as much as arithmetic: a 768-dimensional float32 vector is 3,072 bytes, so 10,000 scattered vectors is about 30 MB of movement before any graph metadata. Batching neighbor vectors contiguously and using SIMD dot products raises throughput. Graph navigation cuts the number of vectors touched; quantisation, later, cuts the bytes per touch. They compose.

4. Trapped At Four

A local minimum is a vertex closer to the query than all of its stored neighbors — and still not the nearest. Widen the candidate queue, or add one bridge edge, and watch the same graph stop lying. Like walking downhill in fog: you reach the bottom of a dip and every direction is uphill, so you stop, one valley short of the sea.

Diagnose a recall miss by naming the mechanism: was the true component unreachable (construction), did greedy stop at a local minimum (breadth, restarts, bridges), did the candidate budget end early (search parameters), or were the distances distorted by the representation? The remedies differ. "Increase the ANN parameter" is not a diagnosis.

5. Highways Then Streets

Ten clusters along a line, target at 9.8. A base-only walk crawls and can be trapped by a weak bridge; a sparse upper layer of long links crosses the corpus first, then descends to refine. Stretch the long links and watch the path collapse. Like driving cross-country: motorway until you're in the right town, then local streets to the door.

Layering does not make search exact. Poor upper links can still route into the wrong region, and weak base connectivity is not repaired by descending. More layers, degree, candidate breadth and construction effort all improve navigability and all cost memory and latency — so recall must be measured against exact neighbors on representative queries, never assumed from the structure.
