Lemmatization maps inflected words to dictionary lemmas instead — was to be, mice to mouse — which avoids some crude suffix errors at higher cost and with a dependence on language and part of speech. A stop list is language- and domain-specific, and it is applied after normalization, which is where it gets dangerous: US has already become us and is about to be deleted as a pronoun. can is a container noun as well as grammar; in a corpus about operating systems, os is not noise. 4. Folding Into Classes

Normalization maps surface strings into equivalence classes before dictionary lookup. Switch the steps on in any order and watch nine surface forms collapse — sometimes usefully, sometimes destructively, and once by accident. Like a shop that files every spelling of a name under one card: quicker to find, until two different people share the card.

Judgment separates display from retrieval: preserve the original text for rendering, citations and offsets — normalized terms are lookup keys, not replacements for source content. Each transformation should be idempotent, applying it twice yielding the same token, and the governing question never changes: which distinctions may safely disappear for this field and this user population?

5. The Analyzer Contract

Every earlier panel was one knob. Here they stack into a single global aggressiveness setting, judged against seven real query classes at once — and then against the escape hatch the whole lesson keeps pointing at. Like one thermostat for a whole building: someone is always too cold.

Judgment starts from product capabilities, not from a default configuration. If exact phrases, names, citations or highlighting matter, retain enough information to support them; if storage is severely constrained and queries are broad topical bags of words, aggressive folding is rational. Evaluate each query class separately — a mean metric hides a total failure on acronyms and proper nouns.
