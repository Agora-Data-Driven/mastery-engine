1. Bytes To Trusted Text

Everything IR 102 has taught so far ends at the same artifact: bytes. Tap each thing you already know and see what it guaranteed — and the one thing none of them touched. Then flip the pipeline switch. Like a courier who gets the crate to your door on time, undamaged, and never once opens it.

A perfect ranker cannot recover meaning erased by a broken PDF extractor, and an LLM will confidently quote a navigation menu if boilerplate dominates its chunks. That is why extraction is a data-quality stage in its own right — typed inputs, versioned parsers, measurable failure signals, and an explicit decision per artifact: accept, retry, quarantine, or discard.

2. Boilerplate Or Content

Link density separates prose from navigation with one ratio. Drag the cutoff, then turn on the template rule that drops anything repeated site-wide — and watch it take something you needed. Like weeding a flowerbed by pulling everything that grows in every bed: fast, and eventually you pull the hedge.

Repetition is a hypothesis of boilerplate, never a proof. A safety warning or a product-version banner repeated site-wide may be essential context, and a unique advertisement evades every repetition rule. Preserve metadata for removed regions, or sample them during quality review, so the rule can be audited instead of trusted.

3. Which Structure Survives

Converting HTML to clean text is a series of decisions about what to keep. Switch each one off and watch the same fragment lose meaning — then pick what the corpus is actually for and see whether it still works. Like transcribing a recipe: drop the line breaks and you still have every word, and nobody can cook from it.

Preserve the minimum structure your declared features need, and err toward a reversible structured representation before lossy flattening — cleaning should remove presentation noise, not semantic organization. Then emit a deterministic content hash and store URL, fetch time, content type, raw hash, extractor version, language and offsets, so a new parser version invalidates chunks deliberately instead of silently.

4. Glyphs Without Order

A PDF stores glyphs at coordinates — reading order, paragraphs and even spaces may be absent. Office files hide a different problem: the value you can see is not the logic that produced it. Pick a source and drive it. Like a ransom note made of cut-out letters: every character is there, and the arrangement is doing all the work.

Security is not a later hardening pass. Documents carry macros, external links, decompression bombs, malformed objects and parser exploits, so parse in isolated workers with CPU, memory, page, file-count and decompressed-size limits; never execute macros or follow external resources; and MIME-sniff the content rather than trusting the filename extension.

5. Accept Or Discard

Quality is multidimensional, so the decision runs off a record, not a glance. Move the four signals — or load the two worked documents — and watch the artifact route itself. Then change what kind of corpus this is. Like triage at a hospital door: the same patient is treated, watched, or sent on depending on what else is in the room.

Downstream impact supplies the final metric: do the accepted documents answer gold queries, preserve cited spans and avoid corrupted chunks? Track rejection and fallback rates by source and extractor version — a sudden spike is an incident — and sample both accepted and rejected cases, because you cannot estimate false acceptance from rejections alone.
