**The big idea**: acquisition is finished. **The Crawl Frontier: Prioritization and Queue Management** decided what to fetch next, **Politeness: Rate Limits, robots.txt, and Crawl Delay** decided how fast, **Crawler Traps and URL Canonicalization** stopped one page arriving under a thousand URLs, **Sitemaps, Feeds, and Change Notifications**, **Search APIs as an Acquisition Channel** and **Seed Selection and Crawl Scope Policy** widened the mouth of the funnel, **Main-Content Extraction and Boilerplate Removal** turned bytes into text, and **Modeling Page Change Rates** with **Recrawl Scheduling and Priority** kept it current. Breadth creates a second problem. The corpus now contains popular pages, isolated gems, copied press releases, SEO farms, and sources of unequal authority — and retrieval scores alone cannot decide what deserves to be indexed or trusted. Corpus quality is four things working together: a graph prior (PageRank), adversarial-content detection, duplicate retention rules, and evidence-backed credibility signals, with enough provenance preserved to audit every exclusion. The shape is one you have already met in **Recency as a Ranking Signal Fused with Relevance**: a query-independent prior is *fused* with relevance, never substituted for it. And where **Extraction Quality Judgments: When to Discard a Document** judged one document on its own, everything here is corpus-level — the evidence for or against a page is the other pages.

**Key concepts**

- **PageRank formalises a recursive intuition about links.** A link is an endorsement; an endorsement from a page that is itself endorsed counts more; and a page linking to hundreds of targets splits its vote among them. For $N$ pages, damping factor $d$ (commonly 0.85) and out-degree $L(v)$: $PR(u)=\frac{1-d}{N}+d\sum_{v\to u}\frac{PR(v)}{L(v)}$. The teleport term $(1-d)/N$ gives every page some probability and lets the random-surfer process restart; the sum transfers rank along incoming links, divided by the source's out-degree.
- **Work one iteration by hand and the mechanism stops being mysterious.** Take A → B and C, B → C, C → A, start uniformly at $1/3$, $d = 0.85$, so $(1-d)/N = 0.15/3 = 0.05$. Then $PR_1(A)=0.05+0.85(1/3)=0.333$, $PR_1(B)=0.05+0.85(\frac{1/3}{2})=0.192$, and $PR_1(C)=0.05+0.85(\frac{1/3}{2}+\frac13)=0.475$. The three values sum to 1, and repeating the update converges to a stationary distribution. C gains because both A and B point at it; B receives only half of A's vote because A split it.
- **A dangling page — no outgoing links — would trap probability if left alone.** Its rank goes in and never comes out, so the mass drains away from every other page and the vector stops being a distribution. The standard fix redistributes a dangling page's rank according to the teleport distribution, commonly uniformly, on every iteration. With sparse adjacency lists one iteration is $O(N+E)$; iterate until the rank change falls below a tolerance, or hit a fixed cap.
- **PageRank is query-independent, and that is its limit as much as its power.** It estimates graph prominence, not relevance to `Firestore transaction retry`. A highly linked homepage must not outrank a precise, low-link API reference merely because site navigation points at it. Use PageRank as a prior or a feature *alongside* lexical relevance.
- **Link graphs are biased, and the bias is structural.** New pages have fewer links; site templates manufacture thousands of non-editorial internal links; isolated specialist sources can be excellent. Compute host-level and page-level signals separately, downweight repeated navigation edges, and distinguish internal from external endorsements. Time-aware graphs reduce stale accumulated advantage — but the result is then no longer classic stationary PageRank.
- **Spam and SEO-junk detection asks one question: is this page built to manipulate acquisition or ranking rather than to satisfy an information need?** Content signals include keyword stuffing, unnatural repetition, very high ad or affiliate density, templated paragraphs with thin unique text, doorway pages differing only by city or product name, hidden text, incoherent machine-generated passages, and title-body mismatch. Link signals include reciprocal rings, sudden low-quality inbound bursts, and many domains sharing ownership or templates.
- **The worked page shows why no single threshold decides.** 2,000 words, of which 1,700 are a repeated template plus city names; `best vector database` appears 120 times; 40% of visible elements are affiliate links; the actual factual section is 80 words. Derived features: unique-shingle ratio 0.12, query-term repetition far above peer pages, link density 0.4, and near-duplicate similarity to 5,000 sibling URLs. Any one of those has an innocent explanation. Together they are strong evidence.
- **Near-duplicate clustering is the sharpest tool against scaled junk.** Generate text shingles, MinHash signatures or normalized hashes; pages with almost identical bodies and a few variable slots fall into one cluster, and you retain a representative only if the template family has value. The signal here is corpus-level repetition.
- **Quality heuristics injure legitimate content, so compare within source type.** API references repeat parameter templates, legal pages use formulaic language, a glossary repeats its key terms, e-commerce pages share layouts by design. Examine unique main content rather than full HTML, and use language-aware models so unfamiliar grammar is not scored as incoherence.
- **The classifier's operating point is a cost decision, not an accuracy one.** Accepting one junk page wastes index space and may pollute answers; rejecting a rare authoritative source can be far worse. Quarantine uncertain high-impact decisions instead of deciding them, and remember that an LLM reviewer is nondeterministic and vulnerable to instructions embedded in the page — treat page text as data, isolate prompts, and cache decisions by content hash *and* model version.
- **Corpus-level dedup policy is about what to keep and what to drop, not about a similarity number.** Duplicates arise from URL parameters, print views, mirrored sites, syndicated articles, document revisions and overlapping chunks. Exact duplicates share a normalized content hash. Near-duplicates share most content after boilerplate removal but may differ in title, date, commentary or one crucial paragraph. Cluster at document grain, then pick a representative by explicit priorities: authoritative original over scraper or mirror; complete content over excerpt; stable canonical URL over tracking or print variant; current valid version over superseded revision; better extraction and metadata; permitted access and durable availability.
- **The worked cluster forces the distinction.** An original research paper, an author-hosted copy, a news summary quoting 60%, and a scraped full copy. Paper and author copy may be exact content duplicates — keep the licensed authoritative version as canonical and record the alternate URL. The news summary is *not* a duplicate: its commentary is a distinct information object despite the quotation overlap. Drop or quarantine the scraper.
- **Dedup is task-dependent, so declare what identity means before you cluster.** "Same bytes", "same work", "same claim", or "same current serving content" are four different policies. A legal archive retains every revision with effective dates; a current-answer RAG corpus serves only the latest but keeps history offline; news analysis needs syndicated copies as evidence of propagation even when the text is identical.
- **Length-aware thresholds, and never the publisher's canonical tag alone.** A shared 100-word footer is a third of a 300-word notice and a rounding error in a 40,000-word manual, so one global threshold mislabels one of them. Canonical tags are publisher hints — they can be wrong or malicious. Store stable cluster IDs, member IDs, similarity evidence, rule version and reason so a representative that disappears can be replaced by promoting another member.
- **Source credibility signals for a research corpus are claim-relative, not permanent.** Credibility is the degree to which a source is appropriate *evidence for a claim*. Signals: provenance (identifiable publisher, author, stable URL); primary-source status; editorial process (peer review, corrections policy, named editors); evidence (citations, accessible data, reproducible methods, explicit uncertainty); currency and version; independence and conflicts; and track record. Store them separately rather than collapsing them into one opaque score, then weight them at query time by intent.
- **The rubric flips with the claim, and that is the whole point.** For a claim about a Cloud Run limit, official versioned documentation scores high on primary-source fit and currency; a two-year-old forum answer offers troubleshooting but lower authority; an affiliate blog copying that forum has weak provenance and independence. For a claim about real-world user experience, the forum supplies evidence the official limit table cannot.
- **Corroboration requires independence.** Five sites copying one press release are one evidence lineage, not five confirmations. Dedup clusters, quoted-source links, publication timing and shared wording expose the dependence; preserve citation graphs and `derived_from` relationships.

**Rules to remember**

- PageRank iterates $PR(u)=\frac{1-d}{N}+d\sum_{v\to u}PR(v)/L(v)$; the mass must sum to 1 every iteration.
- Dangling pages get their rank redistributed by the teleport distribution, or probability leaks.
- A graph prior is a feature fused with relevance, never a substitute for it.
- No single spam threshold proves spam; combinations do, and comparison is within source type.
- Hard-reject only malware, empty extraction and clear policy violations. Probabilistic junk is downranked or quarantined.
- Dedup is a policy: declare the equivalence class, keep one serving representative, preserve alternates and provenance.
- Similarity thresholds must be length-aware and applied after boilerplate removal.
- Credibility is claim-relative, time-relative and evidence-relative; apply ACLs and relevance first, credibility in reranking.
- Independent corroboration means independent lineages, not independent URLs.

**Common pitfalls**

- **Reading PageRank as truth.** It says "structurally prominent in this observed graph", never "factually correct" — and the graph is only what your crawl scope collected.
- **Forgetting the split.** A vote from a page with 100 outlinks is worth a hundredth of a vote from a page with one.
- **Treating SEO as spam.** Descriptive headings, structured data and useful internal links help users and search alike. The distinction is utility and deception, not whether a publisher cares about ranking.
- **Letting one model judgment delete a corpus.** LLM review is nondeterministic and injectable; it advises, it does not execute.
- **Deduplicating by URL canonical tag.** It is a hint from the publisher, not a fact.
- **Using one global similarity threshold.** Short pages cluster on their footers; long ones never cluster at all.
- **Over-deduplicating.** Removing every copy erases independent corroboration — but wire-service text shared verbatim was never independent to begin with.
- **Leaving copies in.** One source then occupies several top ranks, perceived recall inflates, and repeated claims get disproportionate weight in RAG.
- **Collapsing credibility into one number.** You can then neither reweight by claim type nor explain an exclusion.
- **Hiding low-credibility content globally.** It biases research; label it anecdotal instead and expose provenance.

**How to approach the questions**

1. For a PageRank calculation, write the teleport term first ($(1-d)/N$), then add each incoming contribution divided by that source's out-degree. Check that the vector still sums to 1 — if it does not, look for a dangling page.
2. When a question asks whether a signal should *decide* something, the answer is almost always no: PageRank, one spam feature, one similarity score and one credibility number are all inputs to a fused decision.
3. For spam scenarios, ask what source type the page belongs to before judging the feature. The same repetition is damning on a doorway page and normal in an API reference.
4. For dedup questions, first name the equivalence class the corpus has declared. "Is this a duplicate?" is unanswerable until "same work" or "same current serving content" is fixed.
5. For credibility questions, read the *claim*. The source that wins for a specification claim loses for an experience claim.
6. Watch for distractors that convert "not told" into "no": an unreachable signal, a missing link, or an unranked source is missing evidence, not negative evidence.
