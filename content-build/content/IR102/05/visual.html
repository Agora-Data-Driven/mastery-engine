1. Fetched To Trusted

The frontier, politeness, discovery, extraction and recrawl got you 10,000 fetched responses. This lesson is everything that happens after that — and only some of it removes anything. Tap a stage to switch it off and watch the corpus change. Like sorting a delivery: first you throw out the empty boxes, then you notice half of them are the same thing, and only then do you ask who sent them.

Read the ACTION column, not the counts. Crawler Traps and URL Canonicalization settled identity at the URL; Extraction Quality Judgments judged each document alone. Everything added here is corpus-level: the evidence for or against a page is the other pages. And two of the four new stages remove nothing at all — a graph prior reorders, credibility labels. Both are fused with relevance later, exactly as Recency as a Ranking Signal was.

2. PageRank By Hand

Three pages: A links to B and C, B links to C, C links back to A. Start uniform at 1/3, then step the update and read the arithmetic. Move the damping factor, and break the graph on purpose. Like passing a jug of water round a circle: everyone pours what they hold, split evenly among the people they pour to — and a little is topped up from the tap every round.

One iteration over sparse adjacency lists is O(N+E); you iterate until the rank change falls below a tolerance or you hit a cap. The number that comes out is "structurally prominent in this observed graph" — never "factually correct". It is query-independent, so it cannot know that a precise low-link API reference beats a heavily linked homepage for Firestore transaction retry. Use it as a prior, and remember link farms exist.

3. Spam Signal Stack

The worked page: 2,000 words, 1,700 of them a repeated template and city names, best vector database 120 times, 40% of visible elements affiliate links, 5,000 near-identical siblings. Move the sliders — then change what kind of page it is and watch the same numbers mean something else. Like an accent: suspicious in one neighbourhood, completely ordinary in the next.

SEO is not spam. Descriptive headings, structured data and useful internal links help users and search alike — the distinction is utility versus deception, not whether a publisher cares about ranking. And an LLM reviewer can spot incoherence or unsupported claims, but it is nondeterministic, costly and vulnerable to instructions embedded in the page: treat page text as data, isolate the prompt, demand structured evidence, and cache the decision by content hash and model version. Never let one model judgment silently delete a corpus.

4. Keep Or Drop

One cluster, four members: an original research paper, an author-hosted copy, a news summary quoting 60%, and a scraped full copy. The similarity numbers never change — pick what the corpus is for and every verdict moves. Like a family photo album: the archivist keeps every print, the gallery hangs one, the fraud investigator wants the copies precisely because they are copies.

Store the cluster ID, the member IDs, the similarity evidence, the rule version and the reason — then a representative that loses access permission is replaced by promoting another member, not by rediscovering the cluster. Never deduplicate on the publisher’s canonical tag alone: it is a hint, and it can be wrong or malicious. And re-run clustering when extraction improves, because bad boilerplate removal manufactures false similarity.

5. Credible For What

Three sources, seven signals, one Cloud Run question. Credibility is not a permanent number attached to a domain — change the claim and the ranking reorders under fixed signal values. Like asking who to trust about a restaurant: the chef for the recipe, the diner for the queue.

Credibility neither authorises access nor guarantees relevance: apply ACL and scope constraints first, retrieve relevant candidates, then use credibility in reranking or answer policy. Automated ratings encode cultural, language, institutional and popularity bias — a smaller regional source may lack inbound links and polished metadata while holding the primary evidence. So the answer layer exposes provenance and labels rather than an invisible score, and when credible sources conflict it preserves the disagreement and the effective dates instead of blending them into one confident claim.
