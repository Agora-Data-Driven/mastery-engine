1. Sample The Traffic

Precision@k and Recall@k, Average Precision and MAP, Reciprocal Rank and MRR and nDCG all produce ONE number — an average over a set of queries somebody chose. Choose the set here and watch the same change look like a win or a disaster. Like a nationwide poll that only phoned one city: the arithmetic is perfect and the answer is wrong.

"Golden" does not mean perfect or permanent — it means trusted, versioned and deliberately constructed. Two more rules ride on this panel. Never admit a query because the current system succeeded on it: click-gated sampling quietly deletes abandonment and zero-result failures. And keep the set alive — 800 queries as a stable core with 200 refreshed each quarter keeps trend lines interpretable while new feature names and new wording keep arriving.

2. Depth Of The Pool

You cannot judge every document, so you judge the union of what several systems retrieve — and everything you never judged is scored as irrelevant. Switch contributors on and change the depth, then watch a challenger win or lose a comparison it never changed. Like marking an exam where any answer the markers did not read counts as blank.

Two habits fall out of this. Judge blind — hide the contributing system and the rank, or the assessor infers that a top result deserves a high grade and the evaluation just measures the ranking it was meant to test. And record provenance: system versions, retrieval dates, depth per contributor, snapshots. The stopping question is never "is everything judged", it is whether the remaining incompleteness could reverse this decision.

3. Fooling The Judge

An LLM relevance judge is an instrument with calibration error, not an oracle. Change ONE feature of the same document at a time — the paired-counterexample test — and the grade it moves is the bias, measured. Like a job interview where the confident suit outscores the better candidate.

Two more things belong in the prompt, not in your hopes. Define relevance operationally — does topicality, completeness, authority, recency or harmfulness move the grade — with boundary examples and a structured output such as {grade, rationale, evidence}. And version everything: model identifier, prompt, decoding settings, truncation, rubric. A provider update silently relabels your whole collection, so recalibrate before adopting a new judge.

4. Grade Drift Dial

"The judge is one grade off" sounds small. Under exponential gain it is not: grade 2 is worth 3 and grade 3 is worth 7. Move the drift and watch nDCG detach from the truth. Like a thermometer reading two degrees high — harmless for a bath, catastrophic for an oven.

This is why calibration is reported as a confusion matrix. "87 percent agreement" cannot tell you whether the disagreements are 0-versus-1 (nearly harmless) or 2-versus-3 (a 2.33× reweighting of your best documents). And it is why collapsing grades is a real fix rather than a retreat: if the 2/3 distinction is unreliable, a metric that never depends on it cannot be distorted by it.

5. Benchmark Transfer

MS MARCO and BEIR are shared reference points, not proof of production quality. Pick the product you are actually building and see which public evidence survives the trip — tap any row for the mechanism. Like a driving licence earned in Arizona and used in a Norwegian winter.

One last discipline in the reporting itself. State dataset and task version, corpus, query split, metric cutoff, judgment handling and resource budget — a one-point nDCG gain bought with a hundredfold latency increase is irrelevant to production, and two numbers produced under different preprocessing or evaluation scripts were never the same measurement.
