Offline is the cheap gate, online is the verdict, and neither is authoritative alone. The loop matters as much as the order: after an online outcome, go back to the offline set and find which queries predicted it and which contradicted it. That is what improves the evaluation model, instead of leaving you with two unrelated leaderboards.

2. Where Each Lies

Drag the two readings against each other. The interesting cell is never "both agree" — a disagreement is the most informative result an experiment can give you, and each corner has its own list of suspects. Like two witnesses to the same crash: it is where their stories differ that you learn something.

Before you read any corner, prove the experiment happened: assignment integrity, exposure, event schemas, sample ratios, missing data. Interference alone can fake a whole result — caches mix treatment behaviour, returning users cross devices, and a logging difference between variants manufactures a metric change out of nothing.

3. Same Mean, Different Risk

System B is fixed at nDCG [0.6, 0.6, 0.4, 0.4]. Spread system A apart and its mean never moves off 0.500 — but the product does. Then turn on the paired view and watch pairing pay for itself. Like an average water depth of one metre: perfectly safe, right up until you meet the deep end.

Report the median and the quantiles beside the mean, plus how many queries improved, tied and regressed. And remember the metric contributes variance of its own: Reciprocal Rank jumps sharply when the first relevant result moves a single position, while nDCG over several graded documents moves smoothly.

4. Bootstrap And Permutation

One query set of paired deltas — 30% wins near +0.09, 20% losses near −0.08, the rest ties. Resample it live and watch the interval tighten as queries are added, then set the effect you would actually care about. Like polling: asking more people narrows the margin of error, and never tells you the result matters.

Two things no resampling fixes. Dependence: near-duplicate queries or many queries from one session are not independent evidence, so resample by user, session or intent group. And a buggy metric: validate the implementation against hand-calculated cases first, because a confidence interval around the wrong quantity is merely precise misinformation.

5. Slice The Regression

A hybrid retriever's mean nDCG rose 0.015 across 400 queries. Slice it by class, set how many queries you demand before you will call a slice a finding, and tap any row for the root cause. Like a company reporting record profit while one division quietly burns.

Weight by what the business carries, not by query count alone: a class of 500 queries losing 0.02 demands attention even while the overall number rises, and a rare compliance failure can outrank a larger average gain. Keep a preregistered taxonomy — classes invented after seeing the outcome are hypotheses for the next evaluation, not findings in this one.
