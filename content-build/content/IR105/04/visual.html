1. Which Side Moves

The Assembled BM25 Formula, Tuning k1 and b in Practice and Jelinek-Mercer vs Dirichlet Smoothing all tuned how a document is scored against a query that never changed. Pick a side and turn its dial. Like adjusting your glasses versus rewording the question you actually asked.

The whole course so far has been the left button. Relevance feedback is the right one: evidence about which results were useful changes the query representation, so the next ranking expresses the need more fully. Everything after this panel is about doing that without losing what the user actually asked for.

2. Move The Query Vector

Rocchio is geometry: q_m = alpha·q0 + beta·centroid(D_r) − gamma·centroid(D_nr). Drag the three weights over the lesson's own example and watch the query walk across the space. Like steering toward the friends who gave good directions and away from the one who sent you the wrong way.

Document vectors normally use tf-idf or another retrieval-aware weighting; on raw counts, common terms dominate the centroid and the "learned" expansion is noise. Afterwards the ranker typically keeps only the highest-weight expansion terms — for query cost, and so a person can read what changed and undo it.

3. The Interpolation Dial

RM3 estimates a relevance language model from the pseudo-relevant documents and mixes it with the original query model. Drag lambda, then flip which side of the mixture lambda is named after. Like a tap that blends hot and cold — with the labels swapped between two brands of tap.

P(t|q_expanded) = lambda·P(t|q_original) + (1−lambda)·P(t|R)

RM3 must not simply pick the most frequent feedback words. Collection-aware estimation or background correction stops terms that occur everywhere from winning, and stop-word handling, field choice and document length all shape the estimate: titles yield focused terms, full bodies give coverage and noise.

4. Depth Drift Duplicates

Pseudo-relevance feedback assumes the top documents are relevant, which converts ranking errors into expansion evidence. Choose a query, set the feedback depth, and tap any document to drop it. Like asking the first three people you meet for directions and averaging their answers.

Evaluate the whole two-pass system, latency included, and slice by initial retrieval quality — PRF helps when early results are good enough to supply vocabulary and harms when the first pass is wrong. An oracle run over truly relevant feedback documents gives the ceiling; the gap between oracle and pseudo-feedback is the measured cost of selection errors.

5. Anchor Then Expand

Every modern expansion channel is the same move with a different generator: infer a better representation of the need, then retrieve again. Pick a channel, set how much of the original query survives, and add candidates. Like getting a second opinion — worth having, unless all five doctors trained under the same one.

Evaluate three systems, not one: no expansion, oracle expansion, and the deployable thing — tracking top-k relevance, recall, latency, drift rate, exact-match retention and performance by query class. And apply access control before feedback extraction: an expansion derived from documents the user cannot see leaks vocabulary across permissions.
