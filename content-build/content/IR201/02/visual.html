The same factorization you met as a dual-tower recommender: a user tower, an item tower, a dot product to retrieve candidates. Here the query is the user-side context and a passage is the item. It is also why an encoder change is a coordinated migration — vectors from unrelated spaces produce meaningless scores even when the dimensions happen to match, and nothing errors.

2. Pooling And Padding

A transformer gives one contextual vector per token. A sentence embedding is a pooling rule over those states — and the rule has to exclude padding, or the whole index degrades with nothing to show for it. Add padding tokens and watch. Like averaging a team's votes while counting the empty chairs: every real voice gets quieter.

The test that catches this: encode the same text alone and inside a padded batch. With correct masking and a deterministic model the vector should be effectively unchanged. Track vector norms, NaN counts, truncation rates and empty inputs during batch indexing — a silent masking regression rebuilds the entire index successfully while degrading every neighbourhood in it.

3. MaxSim Late Interaction

Keep the token vectors instead of pooling them and each query token can find its own supporting document token. Tap a cell, drag its similarity, and watch the max-per-query-token sum move. Like two people sent to a shelf: each finds their own book, instead of both settling for the shelf's average.

Choose sentence embeddings for high-throughput candidate generation, moderate memory and a strong whole-text signal; choose token-level representations when exact local interactions materially improve recall on long or detail-sensitive text and the infrastructure budget supports them. The decisive evidence is end-to-end quality per unit of latency and memory — never the label sentence or token.

4. Bytes Per Chunk

Fidelity has a bill, and it is arithmetic you can do in your head: vectors × dimensions × 4 bytes for float32. Drag both dials and watch a ten-million-chunk index go from tens of gigabytes to hundreds. Like photocopying a book: one summary page per chapter, or every page — both are "the book", at wildly different filing-cabinet cost.

This is why the standard design is multi-stage: a single-vector retriever finds a manageable candidate set, and a richer interaction model scores only that set. You pay 30.72 GB for the whole corpus and the 128× representation for a few hundred candidates — not for ten million of them.

5. When Metrics Disagree

Cosine, dot product and Euclidean distance are related and not interchangeable. Stretch one candidate's magnitude and watch the podium change under you — then normalise and watch all three agree forever. Like ranking runners by finish time versus by the direction they ran: same athletes, different podium.

Metric selection cannot rescue a weak representation. Trying cosine, dot product and Euclidean on the same unlabelled examples is not model adaptation. Start with the metric prescribed by the model or its training loss, reproduce the preprocessing contract, evaluate retrieval metrics — and change the metric only when evidence supports it, then rebuild the index consistently.
