1. One Dot Product, N Times

Dual-Tower Architecture made a single comparison cheap — one dot product of two 768-dimensional vectors — and Similarity Metrics fixed what "nearest" means. Neither said how many comparisons a query costs. Drag the corpus and watch the same cheap operation become the whole bill. Like a phone call: one is nothing, ten million is a call centre.

Notice what does not happen as you drag: nothing gets asymptotically stupid. Matrix multiplication is superbly optimised and accelerators make exact search viable at real scale. The problem is only that cost and latency grow directly with the corpus — which is why the fix is never a faster loop, it is not scoring most of the vectors at all.

2. The nprobe Dial

IVF is your k-means, used as a router: centroids partition the space, every vector joins a posting list, and a query scans only the nearest cells. Move the two dials and watch how little of the corpus you actually touch — and where that goes wrong. Like a filing room of labelled drawers: you open the two or three that could hold it, not all thousand.

The list count is a build-time decision and it is wrong in both directions: too few leaves large scans, too many gives tiny cells, expensive centroid selection, sparse training and maintenance complexity. And centroids inherit whatever you trained them on — a distribution shift creates overloaded or semantically poor cells, which shows up as recall that varies by segment rather than falling overall. Monitor list-size imbalance, probe counts, recall by segment, and the fraction of results removed by post-filtering.

3. Walking The Layers

HNSW replaces partitions with shortcuts. Upper layers hold few nodes and give long jumps; the bottom layer holds every node and gives local detail. Step the walk, then change the frontier width and watch the same walk end somewhere else. Like flying to the country, then the city, then walking the last street.

M (how many neighbor connections each node keeps) and efConstruction (the breadth used while finding a new node's neighbors) are baked into the graph at build time — more links improve navigability and recall and cost memory and build work. Only efSearch moves at query time, which is why it is the dial in every sweep table. HNSW buys strong low-latency recall with no centroid training phase; it pays in graph memory beyond the raw vectors, expensive construction, deletion and churn maintenance, and metadata filtering that can send traversal through many disallowed nodes.

4. Ninety-Six Bytes

Product quantization splits a vector into subvectors and replaces each one with the ID of a learned centroid. Choose how many pieces and how big each codebook is, and watch the code size, the lookup table and the distortion move against each other. Like describing a face by picking a nose, a chin and a hairstyle from numbered catalogues — a few small numbers instead of a photograph.

PQ almost never travels alone: IVF narrows the searched cells and PQ compresses and rapidly scores their contents, then the best approximate candidates are reranked against original full-precision vectors stored elsewhere — quality recovered, main index still compact. And PQ assumes the chosen subspaces can be quantized usefully, so poorly balanced dimensions waste capacity; rotations such as optimized product quantization redistribute variance more favourably before the split.

5. Pick An Operating Point

Recall, latency and memory are one budget with three claims on it. Set the candidate stage's latency budget, decide whether vectors are stored raw or quantized, and count your replicas — the configuration falls out of the objective, not out of the table. Like packing for a flight: weight, space and what you actually need, and you cannot maximise all three.

Tune end to end, and remember what the recall column is not. ANN recall measures agreement with exact vector search; relevance recall measures whether useful documents reach the candidate set. Going from 0.98 to 0.995 may have no measurable relevance gain if the lost vector neighbors were redundant — and may matter greatly when each query has one unique relevant document. If a reranker needs 100 candidates, ask whether ANN search reliably supplies useful documents within those 100, not whether the first ten approximate neighbors equal the exact first ten. Keep exact-search regression queries so an index upgrade cannot silently exchange relevance for speed.
