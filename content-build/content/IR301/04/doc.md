A RAG system fails in three separable places: retrieval can miss the evidence, the generator can ignore the evidence it was given, and the answer can be well-grounded yet off-target. A single quality score cannot distinguish these, so evaluation must decompose the pipeline. This lesson builds the metric family that has become standard: faithfulness for grounding, context precision and recall for retrieval, answer relevance for the end product, and the tooling landscape with its central trap, the uncalibrated LLM judge.

## Faithfulness: Is the Answer Grounded in the Context

Faithfulness asks whether every factual claim in the answer is supported by the retrieved context. It is the hallucination metric, and it measures grounding discipline, not truth. An answer can be faithful to wrong context, because retrieval failed, or unfaithful yet factually correct, because the model answered from memory. Those are different defects with different owners, and only a claim-level metric separates them.

The measurement mechanism has two steps. First, decompose the answer into atomic claims: the sentence `The Team plan costs 20 per seat and billing is monthly` splits into two claims, one about price and one about billing cadence. Second, verify each claim against the context with an entailment check, asking whether the retrieved text supports exactly this claim. Faithfulness is supported claims divided by total claims. An answer carrying six atomic claims with five supported scores 0.83, and the unsupported claim tells you where to dig: a number absent from the context points to retrieval or memory leakage, while a claim that contradicts the context, annual said as monthly, points to generation.

Claim-level granularity is essential because the dangerous failure is one fabricated number inside a long, fluent, mostly-correct answer. Answer-level averaging hides exactly that case. Numbers, dates, and named entities deserve special attention in decomposition, since they are the highest-risk claims and the easiest for a verifier to check mechanically.

Operationally, faithfulness is sampled continuously on production traffic, not measured once at launch. A healthy deployment looks like this: every night, a few hundred sampled answers are decomposed and verified, and the score is tracked per release, per query class, and per claim type. A drop from 0.94 to 0.88 after a prompt change is a release-blocking signal; a slow slide over a month usually tracks corpus or traffic drift instead. The metric also feeds the reflection gate from the previous lesson: below threshold, the system regenerates, caveats, or abstains. One subtlety: legitimate synthesis, combining two supported facts into a comparative sentence, is supported inference, not fabrication, and an overly strict verifier that penalizes it will train you to ignore the metric. Tuning the verifier's strictness against human labels is therefore part of operating the metric, not a one-time setup. Report faithfulness by claim type and by query class, and expect the LLM verifier itself to be imperfect, which is the subject of the tooling topic below.

## Context Precision and Context Recall

These two metrics evaluate the retrieval half, taking the chunks actually sent to the model as the unit of analysis.

Context recall asks whether the retrieved context contains the evidence needed to answer. It needs a reference, either a gold answer or gold supporting passages, and measures the fraction of required evidence present in the retrieved chunks. This is candidate recall from IR 202, relocated to the RAG boundary: it no longer matters that a fact is somewhere in the index; it matters that the fact is inside the top-k chunks the generator will see. A system with 0.95 corpus recall can still starve the generator if chunking splits the evidence or fusion buries it.

Context precision asks the complementary question: of the chunks sent to the model, how many are actually useful, and are they ranked where the model will use them? The ranking caveat is not cosmetic. Language models are position-sensitive; they exploit the beginning and end of a long context far better than the middle, the lost-in-the-middle effect, so a relevant chunk at position seven of ten behaves partly as if absent. Precision is therefore measured with rank awareness, not just as a fraction.

A worked case: eight chunks retrieved, three relevant, precision 3/8 = 0.375. The five noise chunks are not neutral filler. They dilute attention, and worse, they invite distraction-hallucination, where the model weaves an irrelevant but plausible chunk into the answer. Every irrelevant chunk in the context is a liability. The remedies are the IR 202 toolkit applied at this boundary: rerank harder, cut k, filter by metadata, or drop chunks below a relevance threshold.

The recall side deserves a worked case too. A support question requires two facts: the refund window and the exception for annual plans. Retrieval returns the refund window in chunk two, but the exception lives in a different document section that was never chunked with enough surrounding context to match the query, so it never enters the candidate set. Context recall is 1/2 = 0.5, and the generator, seeing only half the policy, produces a confident half-answer. Notice the chain of custody: corpus recall was fine, since the exception text was indexed, but chunking and retrieval together failed to deliver it to the context boundary. When context recall is low, audit chunking first and retrieval second; the evidence is often present but packaged so that no query can find it.

Without gold data, an LLM can grade each chunk for usefulness, giving a precision proxy; recall still needs a reference answer to test whether the context can produce it. Judgment: precision and recall trade off through k and rerank depth exactly as the funnel predicts, and the right operating point is high precision with sufficient recall, because the generator can only use what it sees and is shaped by everything it sees. Report both by query class.

## Answer Relevance and End-to-End Quality

The third axis evaluates the answer itself, given the question. Answer relevance asks whether the response addresses what was actually asked. A system can retrieve well and ground perfectly and still fail here: the user asks `how do I cancel` and receives a faithful plan comparison. The answer is supported by context and completely useless.

Measurement options: an LLM judge scores whether the answer addresses the question on a rubric, or a reverse-consistency check generates synthetic questions from the answer and compares them to the original, rewarding answers that could only have come from the real question. Both are cheap enough to run on every sampled interaction.

End-to-end quality has more dimensions than relevance, and mature dashboards track them separately. Completeness: every facet of a multi-part question answered. Format compliance: the requested shape delivered, table when asked for a table. Citation accuracy: each citation points at the span that supports it, connecting back to lesson 02's citation grounding, because a correct answer with wrong citations destroys trust as effectively as a wrong answer. And correctness against a gold answer when one exists, graded by rubric, since free-text equivalence resists exact matching.

The ultimate end-to-end signals are online: thumbs ratings, escalation rate to human support, re-query rate, task completion. Offline metrics exist to predict these, and when offline scores rise while escalations rise too, believe the escalations. The organizational point closes the topic: the three metric families have different owners. Context metrics belong to retrieval, faithfulness to the grounding and prompt layer, answer relevance to generation and UX. A single aggregated RAG score destroys the triage value that is the entire point of measuring.

## Evaluation Tooling: RAGAS, DeepEval, TruLens, and the LLM-Judge Pitfall

The tooling landscape, stated conservatively as of early 2026 because these projects move fast and the concepts outlive the APIs. RAGAS is the open-source library that popularized this exact metric family, faithfulness, context precision and recall, answer relevance, computed by LLM judges with reference-free variants for production sampling. DeepEval packages LLM evaluation in a test-framework style, metrics as assertions over datasets, designed to gate releases in CI the way unit tests gate code. TruLens provides feedback functions and instrumentation in the LangChain and LlamaIndex ecosystem, tracking groundedness and context relevance alongside traces. Around them sit observability platforms that bundle tracing with evaluation, LangSmith, Arize Phoenix, Braintrust, and similar. Selection criteria are mundane but decisive: library versus platform, CI gating versus continuous sampling, and whether support data may leave your project boundary at all.

Nearly every metric in this landscape is computed by a model judging text, and that is the pitfall. Four failure modes recur. Self-preference: judges rate outputs from their own model family higher, so the judge must differ from the generator. Verbosity bias: longer, more confident answers score better regardless of correctness, so rubrics must explicitly forbid rewarding length. Shallow agreement: judges reward lexical overlap with context, passing subtly wrong answers that quote the right phrases, which is why numeric claims need targeted verification. Judge drift: a judge model version update moves every metric without any change in your system, so judge versions must be pinned like dependencies.

The calibration discipline is non-negotiable. Before trusting any LLM metric, validate the judge against human labels on a few hundred examples and record the agreement; re-validate whenever the judge or the generator changes; keep a small golden human-labeled set as the immovable reference that never passes through any model; and treat LLM metrics as regression detectors rather than truth. IR 104's experiment discipline applies unchanged: paired comparisons on the same queries, per-segment slices, confidence intervals on deltas.

The architecture that works in practice has three layers. A golden set of one to three hundred queries with human labels gates releases and schema or prompt changes. Continuous LLM-judged sampling of production traffic watches for drift between releases. Online signals, thumbs, escalations, re-queries, arbitrate when the first two disagree. The summary judgment: LLM judges are not a mistake, they are the only scalable measurement option, but a judge is a sensor, and sensors require calibration, version control, and periodic re-validation before their readings mean anything.
