**The big idea**: **Self-Correcting RAG: Reflect, Re-Retrieve, Refine** gave you a loop that regenerates, caveats or abstains when an answer is not good enough — and **Citation-Grounded Extraction: Attributing Spans to Sources** gave you the machinery that ties a sentence back to the span behind it. Both quietly assumed a number that says *good enough*, and this lesson builds it. A RAG system fails in three separable places: retrieval can miss the evidence, the generator can ignore the evidence it was handed, and the answer can be perfectly grounded yet answer a question nobody asked. A single quality score cannot distinguish those, so evaluation must decompose the pipeline exactly the way **Chunk Size and Overlap: The Core Tradeoffs** and **Selecting and Ordering Retrieved Context** decomposed the plumbing. Four families do the work: faithfulness for grounding, context precision and context recall for retrieval, answer relevance for the end product, and the tooling landscape with its central trap, the uncalibrated LLM-judge.

**Key concepts**

- **Faithfulness asks whether every factual claim in the answer is supported by the retrieved context.** It is the hallucination metric, and the mechanism has two steps. First, decompose the answer into atomic claims: the sentence `The Team plan costs 20 per seat and billing is monthly` splits into two claims, one about price and one about billing cadence. Second, verify each claim against the context with an entailment check — does the retrieved text support *exactly* this claim. Then $\text{faithfulness} = \frac{\text{supported claims}}{\text{total claims}}$. Six atomic claims with five supported scores $5/6 = 0.83$.
- **It measures grounding discipline, not truth — and that distinction is the whole diagnostic value.** An answer can be faithful to wrong context, because retrieval failed, or unfaithful yet factually correct, because the model answered from memory. Those are different defects with different owners. The unsupported claim tells you which: a number absent from the context points at retrieval or memory leakage, while a claim that contradicts the context — annual said as monthly — points at generation.
- **Claim-level granularity is essential because answer-level averaging hides the dangerous case.** The failure that matters is one fabricated number inside a long, fluent, mostly-correct answer; graded as a whole it reads as "mostly right". Numbers, dates and named entities deserve special attention in decomposition, since they are the highest-risk claims and the easiest for a verifier to check mechanically.
- **Faithfulness is a production sensor, not a launch checkbox.** A healthy deployment verifies a few hundred sampled answers every night and tracks the score per release, per query class and per claim type. A drop from 0.94 to 0.88 after a prompt change is release-blocking; a slow slide over a month usually tracks corpus or traffic drift. The same number feeds the reflection gate: below threshold, regenerate, caveat or abstain.
- **Supported inference is not fabrication.** Combining two supported facts into a comparative sentence is legitimate synthesis, and an overly strict verifier that penalises it will train you to ignore the metric. Tuning verifier strictness against human labels is part of *operating* the metric, not a one-time setup.
- **Context recall asks whether the retrieved context contains the evidence needed to answer.** It needs a reference — a gold answer or gold supporting passages — and measures the fraction of required evidence present in the retrieved chunks. This is candidate recall from IR 202 relocated to the RAG boundary: it no longer matters that a fact is *somewhere* in the index, it matters that the fact is inside the top-k chunks the generator will see. A system with 0.95 corpus recall can still starve the generator.
- **The worked recall case shows the chain of custody.** A support question needs two facts: the refund window and the exception for annual plans. Retrieval returns the refund window in chunk two, but the exception lives in a document section that was never chunked with enough surrounding context to match the query, so it never enters the candidate set at all. Context recall is $1/2 = 0.5$, and the generator, seeing half the policy, produces a confident half-answer. Corpus recall was fine — chunking and retrieval together failed to deliver. When context recall is low, audit chunking first and retrieval second.
- **Context precision asks the complementary question: of the chunks sent, how many are useful, and are they ranked where the model will actually use them?** Eight chunks retrieved with three relevant gives precision $3/8 = 0.375$. The ranking caveat is not cosmetic: language models are position-sensitive and exploit the beginning and end of a long context far better than the middle — the lost-in-the-middle effect — so a relevant chunk at position seven of ten behaves partly as if absent. Precision is therefore measured with rank awareness, not just as a fraction.
- **The five noise chunks are not neutral filler.** They dilute attention, and worse they invite distraction-hallucination, where the model weaves an irrelevant but plausible chunk into the answer. The remedies are the IR 202 toolkit applied at this boundary: rerank harder, cut k, filter by metadata, or drop chunks below a relevance threshold.
- **Precision and recall trade off through k and rerank depth exactly as the funnel predicts.** The right operating point is high precision with *sufficient* recall, because the generator can only use what it sees and is shaped by everything it sees. Without gold data an LLM can grade each chunk for usefulness, a precision proxy; recall still needs a reference answer. Report both by query class.
- **Answer relevance asks whether the response addresses what was actually asked.** A system can retrieve well and ground perfectly and still fail here: the user asks `how do I cancel` and receives a faithful plan comparison — supported by context and completely useless. Measurement is either an LLM judge scoring against a rubric, or a reverse-consistency check that generates synthetic questions from the answer and compares them to the original, rewarding answers that could only have come from the real question.
- **End-to-end quality has more dimensions than relevance, and mature dashboards keep them apart.** Completeness: every facet of a multi-part question answered. Format compliance: a table when a table was asked for. Citation accuracy: each citation points at the span that supports it — a correct answer with wrong citations destroys trust as effectively as a wrong answer. And correctness against a gold answer, graded by rubric, since free-text equivalence resists exact matching.
- **The ultimate end-to-end signals are online, and they win ties.** Thumbs, escalation rate, re-query rate, task completion. Offline metrics exist to *predict* these; when offline scores rise while escalations rise too, believe the escalations.
- **The three metric families have different owners, which is why aggregation is destructive.** Context metrics belong to retrieval, faithfulness to the grounding and prompt layer, answer relevance to generation and UX. A single averaged RAG score destroys the triage value that is the entire point of measuring — two systems with the same 0.75 can need completely different repairs.
- **Evaluation tooling, stated conservatively: the concepts outlive the APIs.** RAGAS is the open-source library that popularised this exact metric family — faithfulness, context precision and recall, answer relevance — computed by LLM judges, with reference-free variants for production sampling. DeepEval packages evaluation in a test-framework style, metrics as assertions over datasets, designed to gate releases in CI the way unit tests gate code. TruLens provides feedback functions and instrumentation in the LangChain and LlamaIndex ecosystem, tracking groundedness and context relevance alongside traces. Around them sit observability platforms bundling tracing with evaluation: LangSmith, Arize Phoenix, Braintrust. Selection criteria are mundane and decisive: library versus platform, CI gating versus continuous sampling, and whether support data may leave your project boundary.
- **Nearly every metric here is a model judging text, and four judge failure modes recur.** *Self-preference*: judges rate outputs from their own model family higher, so the judge must differ from the generator. *Verbosity bias*: longer, more confident answers score better regardless of correctness, so rubrics must explicitly forbid rewarding length. *Shallow agreement*: judges reward lexical overlap with the context, passing subtly wrong answers that quote the right phrases — which is why numeric claims need targeted verification. *Judge drift*: a judge model version update moves every metric with no change to your system, so judge versions must be pinned like dependencies.
- **Calibration is the non-negotiable discipline, and it is cheap.** Validate the judge against human labels and record the agreement; with $n = 300$ binary labels the 95% half-width is about $0.98/\sqrt{n} \approx 0.057$, so a few hundred is enough to see a bias of 0.1. Re-validate whenever the judge or the generator changes, keep a golden human-labelled set that never passes through any model, and treat LLM metrics as regression detectors rather than truth.

**Rules to remember**

- Decompose before you score. One number for the whole pipeline cannot name an owner.
- Faithfulness is claims supported over claims made, verified by entailment against the context.
- Faithfulness measures grounding, not correctness. Faithful-to-wrong-context and correct-but-unfaithful are both real.
- Context recall needs a reference; context precision can be proxied by an LLM grading chunk usefulness.
- Low context recall: suspect chunking before retrieval.
- Precision is rank-aware because of lost-in-the-middle. A buried relevant chunk is partly absent.
- Every irrelevant chunk is a liability, not neutral padding.
- Grounded plus irrelevant is still a failure — answer relevance is a separate axis.
- Online signals arbitrate. Rising escalations beat rising offline scores.
- A judge is a sensor: validate it against humans, pin its version, re-validate on change.

**Common pitfalls**

- **Reporting one aggregated RAG score.** It moves for four unrelated reasons and points at nobody. Report the families separately, sliced by query class.
- **Grading faithfulness at answer level.** The one fabricated number in a fluent paragraph is exactly what averaging hides.
- **Treating faithfulness as accuracy.** An answer faithful to retrieved-but-wrong context scores 1.0 and is still wrong; that is a retrieval finding, not a grounding one.
- **An over-strict verifier that punishes legitimate synthesis.** The metric becomes noise, the team stops looking at it, and you have lost the sensor.
- **Confusing corpus recall with context recall.** The fact being indexed is irrelevant if it never reaches the top-k the generator sees.
- **Reading context precision as a set property.** Three relevant chunks buried at positions six to eight is not the same system as three at the top.
- **Padding the context "just in case".** Extra chunks lower precision and invite distraction-hallucination.
- **Trusting an uncalibrated judge.** Self-preference, verbosity bias, shallow agreement and drift all move scores without any change to your system — and judging with the same model that generated guarantees a flattering number.

**How to approach the questions**

1. For any "which metric caught this" scenario, locate the defect first: missing evidence is context recall, ignored evidence is faithfulness, wrong target is answer relevance.
2. When a scenario gives claim counts, compute supported over total explicitly. Distractors usually offer the answer-level judgment instead.
3. For precision and recall, check whether the question is about the *set* of chunks or their *order*. Rank-awareness is the frequent hidden clause.
4. If the evidence is described as "indexed but not retrieved", the answer is chunking, not the ranker.
5. When offline and online signals disagree in a scenario, the online signal wins.
6. For tooling questions, match the tool to the *workflow*: CI gating (DeepEval), the metric family itself (RAGAS), tracing-plus-feedback (TruLens).
7. Any judge-related scenario: ask what changed. If nothing in your system changed, suspect judge drift or an unvalidated rubric.
