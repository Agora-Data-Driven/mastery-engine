1. Three Separable Failures

Self-Correcting RAG: Reflect, Re-Retrieve, Refine built a loop that regenerates when the answer is not good enough — but never said what good enough is measured with. Here is that measurement. Break each stage of the pipeline and watch which metric family notices. Like a car that won't start: no fuel, a dead spark, or the right engine driving the wrong wheels — one "check engine" light tells you none of it.

The three families have different owners: context metrics belong to retrieval, faithfulness to the grounding and prompt layer, answer relevance to generation and UX. That is the whole reason to keep them apart — a single aggregated RAG score moves for four unrelated reasons and points at nobody.

2. Counting Atomic Claims

Faithfulness is supported claims over total claims — and the granularity is the whole point. Tap a claim to flip whether the context entails it, and watch what a claim-level score sees that an answer-level judgment never will. Like marking a maths paper line by line instead of writing "looks about right" at the bottom.

Operationally this is sampled continuously, not measured once at launch: a few hundred answers decomposed every night, tracked per release, per query class and per claim type. A drop from 0.94 to 0.88 after a prompt change is release-blocking; a slow slide over a month is usually corpus or traffic drift.

3. Precision Versus Recall

Both metrics take the chunks actually sent to the model as their unit. Move k, rerank, and break the chunking that carries the exception — the two worked cases of the lesson, 0.375 and 0.5, are both in here. Like packing a briefcase for a meeting: too little and you're missing the contract, too much and you can't find it.

Judgment: precision and recall trade off through k and rerank depth exactly as the funnel predicts, and the right operating point is high precision with sufficient recall — because the generator can only use what it sees, and is shaped by everything it sees. Report both by query class.

4. Scorecard Not Score

Three answers to how do I cancel my subscription. Their averaged RAG scores land within a few hundredths of each other; their repairs have nothing in common. Pick an answer, then flip between the aggregate and the scorecard. Like a blood panel: one "health score" is worthless, the twelve rows are the diagnosis.

The ultimate signals are online: thumbs, escalation rate, re-query rate, task completion. Offline metrics exist to predict them — so when offline scores rise while escalations rise too, believe the escalations and go looking for what the rubric is not measuring.

5. Calibrating The Judge

Nearly every metric on the last four panels is a model judging text. Switch on the four biases that recur, and drag the size of the human-labelled golden set that is the only thing able to see them. Like a bathroom scale nobody ever checked against a known weight: it reads consistently, and consistently wrong.

The architecture that works has three layers: a golden set of 100–300 human-labelled queries gates releases and prompt or schema changes; continuous LLM-judged sampling of production traffic watches for drift between releases; online signals arbitrate when the first two disagree. LLM judges are not a mistake — they are the only scalable option. But a judge is a sensor, and sensors need calibration, version control and periodic re-validation before their readings mean anything.
