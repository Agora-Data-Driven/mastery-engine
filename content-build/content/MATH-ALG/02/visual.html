1. Solve Then See

In Modeling with linear equations you read m as a rate and b as a starting amount, and in Number of solutions to equations you solved one equation for one number. Same y = mx + b — now it is a whole line, and the number you solved for is a labelled point on it. Drag m and b. Like a receipt versus the price list: one answer, or every answer at once.

lesson 01 — algebra
y = mx + b is a MODEL m = rate of change b = starting amount at x = 0 solve m·x + b = 0 -> one number
this lesson — geometry
y = mx + b is a LINE m = slope = rise / run set x = 0 -> y-intercept (0, b) set y = 0 -> x-intercept, that same number

One line is missing from this panel and no value of the slider can produce it: the vertical line x = 3. Its run is zero, so its slope is undefined rather than large, and y = mx + b has no way to say it. Every other visual here has to keep that case in mind — it is the exception the forms and the parallel/perpendicular rules all trip over.

2. Rise Over Run

Slope is one ratio of two differences, and every wrong answer on this topic is that ratio assembled wrongly. Drag the two points, watch the triangle, and watch the three broken formulas fail beside the right one. Like a staircase: how much you climb per step forward, not how far you are from the ground.

Order does not matter, consistency does. Taking point 2 first flips the sign of the numerator and of the denominator at the same time, and the two flips cancel. Take the y-difference one way and the x-difference the other and nothing cancels — you get the negative of the truth, which is a perfectly plausible-looking wrong answer.

4. Name That Form

Half the questions on this topic only ask "which form is this?". Tap an equation to see what it is and what it gives you for free — then drag the nudge slider and watch every number change while every form stays exactly what it was. Like recognising a phone number versus a postcode: you know which is which without reading the digits.

The four shapes worth memorising: y = mx + b (slope-intercept), y − y1 = m(x − x1) (point-slope), Ax + By = C (standard) and x = a, the vertical line that only standard form can express, with B = 0. A question naming a form is asking about the template, never about the numbers poured into it.

5. Parallel Or Perpendicular

Two lines, and only their slopes decide the answer. Drag the first slope and watch the four candidates every question is built from — the slope itself, its negative, its reciprocal, and its negative reciprocal — get sorted live. Like two roads: same bearing and they never meet; bearings a quarter turn apart and they cross square.

The product test is the fastest thing you own here: multiply the two slopes and look for −1. A product of +1 means you inverted without negating, and equal slopes with equal intercepts means you are looking at one line twice. Neither of those is a relationship between two distinct lines.
