### Adding, subtracting, and factoring higher-degree polynomials

- Q: Why is factoring a polynomial helpful before solving the equation set equal to zero?
    [ ] It eliminates every constant term
    [ ] It always reduces the degree to 1
    [ ] It guarantees the roots are positive
    [CORRECT] It lets you apply the zero-product property
- Q: Which factoring strategy fits $x^3 - 8$ most directly?
    [CORRECT] Difference of cubes
    [ ] Perfect-square trinomial
    [ ] Applying the quadratic formula
    [ ] Difference of squares
- Q: Factor $x^3 - 27$.
    [ ] $(x - 9)(x^2 + 3x + 3)$
    [ ] $(x + 3)(x^2 - 3x + 9)$
    [ ] $(x - 3)(x^2 - 3x + 9)$
    [CORRECT] $(x - 3)(x^2 + 3x + 9)$
- Q: Factor $x^4 - 16$ completely.
    [ ] $(x - 2)^2 (x + 2)^2$
    [ ] $(x - 4)(x + 4)$
    [ ] $(x^2 - 4)(x^2 + 4)$
    [CORRECT] $(x - 2)(x + 2)(x^2 + 4)$
- Q: Simplify $(3x^3 + 2x - 1) + (x^3 - 5x + 4)$.
    [ ] $4x^3 + 7x + 3$
    [ ] $4x^6 - 3x + 3$
    [ ] $2x^3 - 3x + 5$
    [CORRECT] $4x^3 - 3x + 3$

### Average rate of change of polynomials

- Q: What does the average rate of change of a function on the interval [a,b] represent?
    [CORRECT] The secant-line slope through (a, f(a)) and (b, f(b))
    [ ] The single largest output value the function reaches
    [ ] The slope of the tangent line drawn at the point x = a
    [ ] The y-value of the function at the interval's midpoint
- Q: For $f(x) = x^3 - 1$, find the average rate of change on $[0,2]$.
    [ ] 5
    [ ] 8
    [CORRECT] 4
    [ ] 3
- Q: For $f(x) = x^2$, find the average rate of change on $[1,3]$.
    [ ] 3
    [CORRECT] 4
    [ ] 8
    [ ] 2
- Q: For $f(x) = 2x^2 + x$, find the average rate of change on $[ -1, 1]$.
    [ ] 2
    [ ] 4
    [ ] 0
    [CORRECT] 1
- Q: Which expression gives the average rate of change of f from x = a to x = b?
    [ ] f'(a)
    [ ] f(b) - f(a)
    [ ] (f(a) - f(b)) / (a + b)
    [CORRECT] (f(b) - f(a)) / (b - a)
