### Polar form of complex numbers

- Q: Write $1 + i$ in polar form using its principal argument.
    [ ] $\sqrt{2}(\cos(\pi/2) + i \sin(\pi/2))$
    [ ] $1(\cos(\pi/4) + i \sin(\pi/4))$
    [CORRECT] $\sqrt{2}(\cos(\pi/4) + i \sin(\pi/4))$
    [ ] $2(\cos(\pi/4) + i \sin(\pi/4))$
- Q: Which expression gives the polar form of a complex number with modulus r and argument θ?
    [ ] cos θ + i sin θ (no scale)
    [ ] a + bi (rectangular form)
    [ ] r + θi (radius plus angle)
    [CORRECT] r(cos θ + i sin θ)
- Q: What is the modulus of the complex number $-2i$?
    [ ] $\pi/2$
    [ ] -2
    [ ] 0
    [CORRECT] 2
- Q: Which angle is the principal argument of the complex number $-1 + 0i$?
    [ ] $0$
    [ ] $-\pi/2$
    [ ] $\pi/2$
    [CORRECT] $\pi$
- Q: Which two quantities together determine the polar form of a nonzero complex number?
    [ ] Distance and midpoint value
    [ ] Real part and imaginary part
    [ ] Real part and conjugate
    [CORRECT] Modulus and argument

### Multiplying/Dividing complex numbers in polar form

- Q: Multiply 2(cos α + i sin α) by 3(cos β + i sin β).

    [ ] 5(cos(α+β) + i sin(α+β))
    [ ] 6(cos(α-β) + i sin(α-β))
    [CORRECT] 6(cos(α+β) + i sin(α+β))
- Q: If $z$ has modulus $5$ and argument $\pi/4$, what are the modulus and argument of $z^2$?

    [ ] $10$ and $\pi/2$

    [CORRECT] $25$ and $\pi/2$

    [CORRECT] They are added together
    [ ] They each stay the same
    [ ] They are multiplied together
    [ ] They are subtracted apart
- Q: Divide $6 \operatorname{cis}(\pi/3)$ by $2 \operatorname{cis}(\pi/6)$.
