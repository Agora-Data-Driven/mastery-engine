    [ ] the difference of the row totals
    [ ] the total of a single column
    [ ] the total of a single row
    [CORRECT] the sum of all four cells
- Q: Why are the row and column totals (the margins) of a two-way table useful?
    [ ] They are always guaranteed to add up to exactly 100
    [CORRECT] They let you compare categories and find conditional proportions
    [ ] They establish that one variable directly causes the other
    [ ] They replace the individual cell counts as the primary data

    [ ] 5/20

    [ ] 6
    [ ] 24
    [CORRECT] 30
    [ ] 18

### Distributions in two-way tables (khanacademy.org)

- Q: Which quantity is needed to compute the conditional distribution of the outcome given a particular group?
    [ ] the grand total only
    [ ] the mean of all the cells
    [ ] the range of the data
    [CORRECT] the total for that group
- Q: When two groups have very different sizes, why compare their conditional distributions rather than the raw counts?
    [ ] Because the group totals have no effect on comparisons
    [ ] Because converting counts to percentages establishes causation
    [ ] Because raw counts are inherently misleading in every case
    [CORRECT] Because conditional distributions adjust for the differing group totals
- Q: A conditional distribution computed by row reports
    [CORRECT] percentages within each row
    [ ] percentages within each column
    [ ] the mean of each row
    [ ] raw cell counts only
- Q: Among 20 boys, 8 prefer soccer. Within the boys' row, what is the conditional percentage who prefer soccer?
    [ ] 20%
    [ ] 80%
    [ ] 50%
    [CORRECT] 40%
- Q: If the conditional distributions of one variable are nearly the same across the categories of the other, that suggests
    [CORRECT] little or no association
    [ ] a strong association
    [ ] a nonlinear relationship
    [ ] a perfect correlation
